4. EU Regulation EC 261/2004 Compensation Tiers Breakdown

Exact statutory compensation amounts mandated by European Union law

Monetary compensation under European Union Regulation EC 261/2004 is non-negotiable and fixed by statutory law. Unlike vouchers or miles, compensation must be paid in cash, bank transfer, or check. The exact compensation amount is determined strictly by the total flight route distance and the length of arrival delay at your final airport:

Flight Distance Category Flight Distance (km) Minimum Delay Required Statutory Cash Compensation
Type 1: Short-Haul Up to 1,500 km 3+ Hours at Destination €250 per passenger
Type 2: Medium-Haul / Intra-EU 1,500 km to 3,500 km (or all intra-EU > 1,500km) 3+ Hours at Destination €400 per passenger
Type 3: Long-Haul Extra-EU Over 3,500 km (Non-EU origin/destination) 3 to 4 Hours Delay €300 per passenger (50% reduction)
Type 3: Long-Haul Extra-EU (Full) Over 3,500 km 4+ Hours at Destination €600 per passenger
Geographic Scope of EU261 & UK261 Regulation:

EC261 applies to all flights departing from any airport located inside the European Union (EU), Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom (UK261), regardless of which airline operates the flight. Furthermore, it applies to all flights arriving into an EU/UK airport if operated by an EU or UK-certified airline (e.g., Lufthansa, Air France, Ryanair, Finnair, British Airways).

10. Important Flight Eligibility Conditions & Time Limits

Crucial statutory rules determining whether your disrupted flight qualifies

To successfully claim compensation through Lennuabi under Regulation EC 261/2004, your disrupted flight must meet specific legal prerequisites established by European courts:

1. Departure or Carrier Requirement: The flight must either depart from an airport located inside an EU member state (or UK/Norway/Switzerland) OR arrive into an EU airport operated by an EU-licensed carrier (e.g., Lufthansa, SAS, Ryanair, Wizz Air).
2. Arrival Delay Threshold: Delay compensation is legally triggered by the arrival time at your final destination gate (when aircraft doors open), NOT departure gate delay. The threshold is 3 hours or more for most flights.
3. Statutory Limitation Periods (Time Limits by Country): You can claim for past flight disruptions retrospectively. The limitation period varies by European jurisdiction:
  • Estonia / Germany / Finland: 3 years from the end of the calendar year of travel.
  • United Kingdom: 6 years from the flight date.
  • Spain / France: 5 years from the flight date.
